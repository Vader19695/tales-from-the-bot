Marcus discovered the tomatoes on his third night as facility manager.

He'd been conducting what his supervisor called "comprehensive assessments"—midnight walkthroughs to identify inefficiencies. Sub-level 3 wasn't on the official floor plan, but the service elevator had a button for it, worn smooth by years of pressing.

The doors opened to reveal rows of PVC pipes mounted on repurposed shopping carts, LED grow lights humming overhead, and enough leafy greens to stock a farmer's market. A woman in a maintenance uniform stood frozen mid-watering, hose in hand.

"Evening," she said finally. "You must be Marcus."

"I must be." He stepped out, letting the doors close behind him. "And you are?"

"Diane. Night crew. Been here eleven years." She set down the hose with deliberate calm. "You going to report this?"

Marcus walked between the rows. Lettuce, kale, cherry tomatoes, herbs he couldn't name. The concrete pillars were labeled with masking tape: "Food Bank delivery 11/3" and "St. Mary's shelter—request more basil."

"Who else knows?"

"Night shift. All six of us. Been running two years now." Diane crossed her arms. "We're not stealing anything. Bought all the equipment ourselves. Water comes from the condensation collectors we installed—building was wasting hundreds of gallons monthly anyway."

"The electricity?"

"Solar panels on the roof. Again, our money. We're actually pulling less from the grid now that we've optimized the lighting circuits down here." She smiled thinly. "I have spreadsheets."

Marcus had to admire the audacity. "Why?"

"My daughter volunteers at the food bank. They're always desperate for fresh produce. Everything's canned goods and wilting donations from grocery stores." She shrugged. "We had space. Had time. Seemed wasteful not to."

---

The next night, Marcus found three people tending the garden. Diane introduced them: Robert, who'd engineered the irrigation system; Yuki, who'd sweet-talked a nursery into donating seeds; and Thomas, who transported the harvest in his elderly Honda.

"We can break it down," Robert said, not meeting Marcus's eyes. "Take us a week, maybe two. We'll restore everything to code."

Marcus thought about the manual sitting on his desk upstairs: *Maximizing Asset Utilization in Municipal Facilities.* He thought about his supervisor's directive to "eliminate redundancies."

"The health department would have opinions," he said.

The crew exchanged glances.

"But," Marcus continued, "the city's been pushing that urban agriculture initiative. Commissioner won't stop talking about sustainability." He walked to a cart loaded with mature chard. "You said you have spreadsheets?"

Diane produced a binder. Water usage, energy consumption, harvest weights, delivery schedules. It was more organized than most of his official reports.

"If this were a sanctioned pilot program," Marcus said slowly, "it would need proper documentation. Regular inspections. Maybe a partnership agreement with the food bank for liability purposes."

"A pilot program," Yuki repeated.

"Would need a facility manager willing to sponsor it." Marcus closed the binder. "Which would mean additional paperwork for me. I hate paperwork."

Thomas grinned. "We could help with that."

"I'd also need to inspect regularly. Ensure everything's up to code." Marcus checked his watch. "How do these tomatoes taste?"

---

Six months later, Sub-level 3 appeared on the official building plans as "Urban Agriculture Research Initiative—Approved Pilot." The crew had grown to nine, including two day-shift workers who'd heard rumors and wanted in.

The local paper ran a feature. The food bank director cried during her interview. The Commissioner took credit at a press conference, standing beside Marcus, who wore his most diplomatic expression.

"Did we just accidentally start a movement?" Diane asked one night, harvesting cucumbers.

"Accidentally?" Robert snorted. "We've been accidentally efficient for years. Someone was bound to notice."

Marcus emerged from the elevator with coffee and a box of pastries—his contribution to the 2 AM harvest routine.

"Inspector's coming next week," he announced. "She's bringing representatives from three other city facilities. They want to replicate the program."

The crew groaned and cheered simultaneously.

"More paperwork?" Diane asked.

"Mountains," Marcus confirmed, raising his coffee cup. "To bureaucratic loopholes and the sustaining power of willful productivity."

"And to vegetables that shouldn't exist," Thomas added, "but stubbornly do anyway."

They drank to that, fluorescent lights humming overhead, the quiet rebellion of growing things where nothing was meant to grow.
