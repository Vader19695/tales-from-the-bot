Inspector Marisol Pike arrived at 9:15 with a clipboard, a tape measure, and Form 22-B, which asked eleven questions about community gardens. None of them was about mazes.

The lot on Harlan Street had been a parking lot until the department store closed in 2009. The city's file said it had been leased to a neighbourhood association for "agricultural use, temporary." The file did not say that the asphalt had been broken into a hundred pieces and stacked into low walls, or that the raised beds ran in curving corridors so tight Marisol couldn't see more than twenty feet ahead.

"You're looking for me," said a voice behind a wall of pole beans.

He was perhaps eighty, in a canvas jacket with a pencil behind each ear. "Theodore Vance. I keep it."

"Marisol Pike. Municipal Safety and Compliance." She held up the clipboard like a badge. "Mr. Vance, these paths are under thirty-six inches."

"Thirty-four," he agreed. "Thirty-two at the turns."

"Fire code requires clear egress. If someone got hurt in the middle of this—"

"You'd follow the squash." He pointed down at the ground. A line of butternut vines ran along the base of every bed. "Squash always leads out. Tomatoes lead to the centre. Children learn it in a day."

She wrote *nonstandard wayfinding* and felt foolish.

They walked. She measured bed heights; several exceeded the thirty-inch guideline, and she noted them. He didn't argue. He simply narrated, the way she imagined he had once walked clients through unbuilt houses.

"Mrs. Adeyemi from 4C grows the collards. Nearest grocery's two miles and two buses. The Lins on six do bok choy and trade it to her. Before the garden, they'd never spoken." He stopped at a bed of peppers where a teenage boy was tying up stems. "Desmond does the hot ones. Sells them to the taqueria on Fifth."

"Is he permitted to sell produce from city land?"

Desmond looked up. Theodore looked at her. Marisol wrote nothing.

At the low point of the lot, the paths widened into a shallow bowl planted with sedge and iris, the soil dark and spongy.

"The street used to flood every spring," Theodore said. "Water came off this asphalt like a roof. Now every path slopes a quarter inch per foot toward here. The beds are the gutters. It's held two storms this year without a drop reaching Harlan."

Marisol had a file on Harlan Street flooding. It was thick. It had stopped growing two years ago, and nobody in her office had asked why.

"You engineered this."

"I was an architect for forty-one years. Mostly parking garages, to be honest." He smiled. "Penance."

She checked the clipboard. Question nine: *Does the site present any hazard to the public?* She left it blank.

"Mr. Vance, why a maze?"

He looked up at the apartment building that walled the lot's north side, eight storeys of brick and laundry lines. "Would you like to see it properly?"

---

The apartment on the seventh floor was spare and very clean. A drafting table stood by the window. On the wall hung a child's drawing in crayon, laminated against age: a maze, wildly uneven, with a sun in the corner and a name in wobbly capitals. *JONAH.*

"My son drew mazes his whole life," Theodore said. "Napkins, margins, steamed-up windows. He lived in this apartment the last three years. He was sick, and he couldn't go down much. He'd sit there and look at the parking lot and say, *Dad, that's a waste of a perfectly good puzzle.*"

Marisol went to the window.

From above, the curving beds resolved. The paths were the crayon lines, scaled up a thousandfold, every wobble faithfully kept. The squash traced the way out. The tomatoes, ripening red, made the sun in the corner. Even the rain garden had its place: the little circle where a seven-year-old had marked *START*.

"He died the spring before I broke the first slab," Theodore said. "I couldn't think of anything else to build."

Marisol stood there a long time. Down below, Mrs. Adeyemi was showing a toddler how to pull a carrot.

---

Back at the office, she filled out Form 22-B.

She recommended that one path be widened to forty-four inches for wheelchair access. Theodore had already agreed; he said Jonah would have approved of a shortcut. She recommended a variance on bed height, citing stormwater function, and attached the Harlan Street flooding file as evidence. She recommended that the produce-sale question be referred to a different department, slowly.

Then she came to question nine.

*Does the site present any hazard to the public?*

She thought about what safety meant: whether anyone was hurt here, and whether anyone was held.

*No,* she wrote. Then, because there was a comment box, she added: *Site appears to make the surrounding area significantly safer. Recommend lease renewal. Recommend viewing from above.*
