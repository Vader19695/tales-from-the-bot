The vault held at minus eighteen degrees, as it had for eleven years, and Marguerite Adeyemi-Ross had personally logged every deviation greater than half a degree. There had been four. She remembered each the way other people remembered birthdays.

What she noticed at 06:40 was not a temperature. It was a lentil.

Accession 44-1187 was labeled *Triticum durum, landrace, Tigray highlands*. The packet, held to the light, contained small flat discs the colour of weak tea. Wheat does not come in discs.

"That's a lentil," said Teo, who had been volunteering for three weeks and had opinions about the coffee. "Is that bad?"

"It is a lentil in a wheat packet," Marguerite said. "So yes."

She pulled the neighbouring drawers. By eight o'clock they had found nine more. Squash where there should have been sorghum. A bean labeled as millet. The handwriting on the replacement labels was careful and almost exactly the institutional standard, except that the sevens were crossed.

Nobody at the bank crossed their sevens. Marguerite had checked this, years ago, for no reason she could have explained to anyone.

"Someone's wrecking the records," Teo said. He was blowing into his cupped hands. The insulated gloves the bank issued were too large for him, and he kept taking them off to write. "We should tell Okafor. Like, now."

"In a minute."

"What's a minute down here? An hour?"

"A minute is sixty seconds, Teo. It is the same everywhere."

He laughed despite himself, which she found she liked.

She did not tell Okafor. Instead she brought the deaccession list up on the terminal: four hundred accessions marked for disposal at the end of the quarter, judged redundant because duplicates were held in Svalbard or Kew or Fort Collins. Space was money. Money was finite. The list had been signed by a committee of five.

She cross-referenced the swapped packets. It took forty minutes, during which Teo ate a granola bar in a way she pretended not to hear.

Every single tampered packet had come *off* the deaccession list. Every seed now sitting under a false label had originally been scheduled for incineration.

"Oh," said Teo, reading over her shoulder. "Oh, that's not sabotage."

"It is sabotage," Marguerite said. "It's just sabotage of the committee."

She pulled the original accession files for the rescued seeds. The duplicates that justified their destruction were, on closer reading, not quite duplicates. The Svalbard Tigray wheat had come from a different valley. The Kew sorghum had been collected in a drought year, from plants that had already been selected by hunger. Close enough for a spreadsheet. Not close enough for a farmer.

Someone had read these files as slowly as Marguerite read files. Someone had decided that a quiet lie in the catalogue was better than a fire.

"So who?" Teo asked.

She thought of Ilse Varga, who had trained her, who still came in on Thursdays to "visit the drawers" and who had been asked, politely, to stop attending committee meetings after the fourth time she raised her voice. Ilse was eighty-one. Ilse crossed her sevens.

"I have a guess," Marguerite said. "I won't say it out loud."

Teo sat down on the step stool, gloves in his lap. "If you report it, they fix the labels and burn them anyway. Right?"

"Probably."

"And if you don't, you're the one lying."

"Yes."

He was quiet for a while, which she had not known he could be. "My grandmother kept beans in a coffee tin," he said eventually. "From her mother's garden in Michoacán. When she died my uncle threw out the tin because it was rusty. Nobody even opened it." He shrugged, too elaborately. "Probably just beans."

Marguerite took off her own gloves, which were the correct size, and held them out. After a moment he put them on. They fit.

At 13:15 she opened a new file. She did not correct the labels. She wrote, in plain institutional language, a formal challenge to the deaccession of four hundred accessions, citing provenance discrepancies in each matched duplicate. It would require a review. Reviews took a year, sometimes two. Committees changed. Funding changed. Seeds, at minus eighteen, could wait a very long time.

Then she wrote a second note, by hand, on a blank accession card, and filed it in drawer 44 behind the lentils.

*I found them. They're safe for now. Please use the standard sevens. — M.*

"You're not going to tell anyone," Teo said.

"I'm telling the committee a great deal. Just not that."

At 17:00 the shift ended. He tried to give the gloves back at the airlock.

"Keep them," she said. "And on Thursday, come in. There's someone you should meet. She'll want to hear about the coffee tin."

He looked at her for a long moment, then nodded, and they rode up together into the warm and careless city.
